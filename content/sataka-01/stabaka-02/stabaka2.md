## Śloka 38: The Power of the Unborn Source

>yad ekatvena saṃpadyate tad anyatvena vyāvartate; yad anyatvena saṃpadyate tad ekatvena vyāvartate. Na tatra bhedena bhedena vyavahāraḥ, ananyena vyavahāraḥ. Yatra bhedaḥ sa bhedena vyavahāro bhavatu, ataḥ anirvacanīya eva. Iha tu satkārya-vādino-bhedena vyavahāraḥ na bhavatu, ananyena vyavahāraḥ. Na tat paramārtha-sat eva, anirvacanīya eva. Ataḥ anirvacanīya eva. Na tatra bhedena bhedena vyavahāraḥ, ananyena vyavahāraḥ. Yatra bhedasya bhedasya vyavahāraḥ, anirvacanīya eva.

### Analysis

This śloka delves into the heart of Advaita Vedanta metaphysics, exploring the paradox of identity and difference, unity and multiplicity. The core assertion is that any attempt to define or categorize existence inevitably collapses into contradiction.

* **Identity and Difference**: The text begins with a recursive statement: "What is unified becomes differentiated, and what is differentiated becomes unified." This highlights the impossibility of fixing any reality in a static state. Any attempt to define something as "one" (eka) necessitates positing something else as "other" (anya), which immediately undermines the initial unity. This interplay suggests a dynamic, fluid reality that resists conceptualization.

* **The Limit of Language**: The śloka emphasizes that "there is no transaction with difference-through-difference." Language, which relies on differentiation to function, fails to capture the true nature of reality. Any attempt to describe existence through distinct categories leads to contradiction, as "difference-through-difference is unspeakable." This points to the ultimate ineffability of the Absolute (Brahman).

* **Critique of Satkāryavāda**: The text critiques the Satkāryavāda, the doctrine that the effect pre-exists in the cause. While Satkāryavādins attempt to reconcile unity and multiplicity through causal relationships, the śloka argues that this, too, is ultimately "unspeakable." The very notion of a "real cause" (sat eva) that gives rise to effects through differentiation is seen as a conceptual construct that fails to grasp the ultimate reality.

### Interpretation

This śloka can be interpreted as a refutation of all dualistic and even some monistic metaphysical systems that attempt to provide a definitive account of reality. The constant oscillation between unity and difference suggests that ultimate reality transcends all conceptual categories. Any attempt to define existence through fixed identities or relationships inevitably leads to contradiction.

The śloka's critique of Satkāryavāda is particularly significant. It suggests that even attempts to reconcile unity and multiplicity through causal relationships ultimately fail to capture the true nature of reality. The notion of a "real cause" that gives rise to effects through differentiation is seen as a conceptual construct that fails to grasp the ultimate reality.

Ultimately, this śloka points to the limitations of language and conceptual thought in grasping ultimate reality. The constant oscillation between unity and difference suggests that ultimate reality transcends all conceptual categories. Any attempt to define existence through fixed identities or relationships inevitably leads to contradiction.

### Philosophical Significance

This śloka has profound philosophical implications, particularly for understanding the limitations of conceptual thought and the nature of ultimate reality. It suggests that:

* **Ultimate reality is beyond conceptualization**: The constant oscillation between unity and difference suggests that ultimate reality transcends all conceptual categories. Any attempt to define existence through fixed identities or relationships inevitably leads to contradiction.

* **Language is inadequate for describing reality**: The śloka emphasizes that "there is no transaction with difference-through-difference." Language, which relies on differentiation to function, fails to capture the true nature of reality. Any attempt to describe existence through distinct categories leads to contradiction, as "difference-through-difference is unspeakable."
